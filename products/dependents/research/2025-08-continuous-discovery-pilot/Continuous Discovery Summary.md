In the fourth quarter of 2025 the Lifestage Benefits- Dependents Management team ran a pilot of a research method called Continuous Discovery. We worked with our PO and OCTO’s Research Lead to get buy-in and permission to run this pilot because it differed from OCTO’s regular processes for research with Veterans. Here is a summary of the pilot.

**What is continuous discovery?**

Continuous discovery is the process of product teams regularly talking to users to uncover their needs and validate assumptions. It uses frequent, small-scale research activities as opposed to single, larger studies. The value of continuous discovery is reducing risk of going in the wrong design direction, better aligning with user needs, improving understanding of user needs, and faster input for decision making.

**Why did our team try a continuous discovery approach to research?**

Our goal was to improve usability and trust in dependents-related experiences by making Veteran feedback more frequent, lightweight, and actionable. 

**Benefits of Continuous Discovery:**

- Increased access to Veteran feedback  
  - Veteran feedback could generally be obtained in two weeks. This is about 50% of the time needed to complete a study using VA’s traditional research methods  
  - Newfound speed and access led to increased incorporation of Veteran perspectives into decision-making processes  
- Increased research flexibility  
  - Can repeat studies across multiple cohorts when more data is needed to be confident in a design direction  
  - Can pivot quickly to higher-priority questions as project needs shift   
  - Enables rapid testing of multiple design iterations for frequent course-corrections

**Challenges of Continuous Discovery:**

- Not a good fit for all research questions:   
  - Worked best for certain types of research, such exploratory research and concept testing with lightweight prototypes   
  - Would be difficult in cases where the research required more preparation, such as creating fully-featured prototypes for comprehensive usability testing, coded prototypes for testing with assistive tech users, or recruiting hard-to-reach Veteran groups  
- Limited opportunities for communication:   
  - Communication and next steps remained clear within the HCD team due to frequent research session observation and teams meetings  
  - Broader communication was more challenging in the tight cycle timeline. (The pilot did overlap with the government shutdown, our previous VA HCD Lead departed mid-pilot, and onboarding a full-time successor was delayed by the government shutdown, which were factors as well.) Based on feedback, our team began to present continuous discovery updates in sprint reviews, which improved team-wide knowledge   
  - Summaries of facts and findings were uploaded to GitHub for each cohort, but full reports couldn't be created due to time constraints  
  - Decisions such as what topic to research were made by a small group of team members, as there was not time to seek consensus with the larger group. Having a research backlog helped by giving the larger team a chance to contribute research ideas and giving the researchers quick access to high-priority research questions 

**Next steps:**

- Continue using the Continuous Discovery process when appropriate  
- The team has made updates to the Continuous Discovery process that we used for the pilot to address some of the challenges.
