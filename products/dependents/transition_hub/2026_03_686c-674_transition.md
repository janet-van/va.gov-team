## Product context

- The online **21-686c** form allows Veterans to add or remove dependents to/from their VA disability compensation or pension benefits. If a Veteran is supporting a dependent, they may be eligible to receive additional disability or pension compensation.  
  - [About VA Form 21-686c (Application Request to Add and/or Remove Dependents)](https://www.va.gov/find-forms/about-form-21-686c/)  
  - Launched on VA.gov: May 2018 with 674 form flow  
  - 686c annual submission volume: 342,750 (2024)  
  - Current pdf version on VA.gov: AUG 2025  
- The online form flow for the **21-674** form (Request for Approval of School Attendance) is integrated with the 21-686c form. The 674 form is used to extend benefits to a Veteran’s child if they are between 18-23 years old and attending school full time. Normally, a minor child dependent ages out of benefits when they turn 18 unless a 674 is filed indicating the child is in school.  
  - [About VA Form 21-674 (Request for Approval of School Attendance)](https://www.va.gov/find-forms/about-form-21-674/)  
  - Launched on VA.gov: May 2018 with 686 form flow  
  - 674 annual submission volume: 73,300 (2024)  
  - Current pdf version on VA.gov: NOV 2024

### Product Goal

Make the online dependent experience as easy to complete and cohesive as possible, while ensuring that Veterans verify dependent information on a regular basis and that submissions are reliable and processed as quickly as possible.

- The long term goal is that Veterans can come to the dependent management area of VA.gov and add/remove their dependents (21-686c) and/or request approval of their school-aged child (21-674) within a single form flow experience. Many of these forms ask for the same information, so there is a lot of opportunity to leverage a single form experience to prefill information and reduce the burden on the Veteran to manage their dependents.

## Context for WIP design work

### Lifestage Mobile App exploration

**Problem it solves**  
Veterans often lose track of how current their dependents are, and if not updated, it could lead to overpayments or underpayments. The VA Health and Benefits Mobile app (frequently mentioned by our research participants) could provide another place to surface current dependent information to Veterans.

**What we did**  
We did a [discovery effort](https://app.mural.co/t/departmentofveteransaffairs9999/m/departmentofveteransaffairs9999/1755642960566/2825754c5b58bb972a5a7f5a594b46d982e2ad58?wid=0-1763676976679) of the current mobile app so we could understand what, if anything, existed from a dependent, pension, or burial perspective. Then we mapped out an [initial flow](https://app.mural.co/t/departmentofveteransaffairs9999/m/departmentofveteransaffairs9999/1745620624724/70ee36c200a3b66b426c33aa189dcdcc5a4a0192?wid=0-1773779329130) of what a dependent experience might look like in the mobile app.

**Other notes**

* The design team hypothesized that putting dependent information in the mobile app would increase Veterans interacting and updating with dependent information. That could mean fewer overpayments. Also, the mobile platform could be an easy way to send push notifications to Veterans when dependent information needs updating.

**Why it was stopped**  
The team progressed this until the end of the contract period.

**Next steps**

* The team met with the Mobile team (Ryan Thurwell) to do a handoff and knowledge transfer for the work we've completed on March 20, 2026\. That team will take the design work, take it further and do the implementation. It's on their roadmap for later in 2026\.

### 686c 674 Per Dependent Evidence Upload Experience

**Problem it solves**  
Currently, the form waits until the final upload step to list required evidence if evidence requirements were triggered based on responses within the form. The upload step has one screen for spouse evidence uploads and one screen for children. The child upload screen does not list evidence specific to each dependent and requires users to infer which document corresponds to which person. In cases where a Veteran is adding multiple dependents at once (e.g., a new child, a marriage), it can become unclear which document supports which part of the form. Additionally, the evidence requirement trigger for adding a spouse if the claimant lives outside the US is not working. In that scenario, the claimant should see the upload screen with evidence instructions, but the spouse evidence upload screen does not show.

**What we did**  
Most recently, we conducted research on a design exploration around uploading and clarifying evidence. Based on that research, we did some more design explorations, focused on informational upload page as a concept. We created some explorations and documented the context and history around this area and what needs to be considered so the next team can make design decisions.

**Other notes**

### Phase 1 of Add flow work: Assorted child add improvements

The team began Add flow enhancement explorations and work after the removal picklist design work completed. As the team progressed, we decided to break up the work into phases so it could be released to Veterans more efficiently. Phase 1 changes represent the simpler changes, which require no Collaboration Cycle process and no research with Veterans. Each of these is in various stages in the software development lifecycle, and each could be independently released (there are no dependencies with other form sections).

**Problem it solves**  
The features in this initiative are UX enhancements to different sections of the ‘Add child flow in the 686\. These changes cover a number of areas, including:

* Stepchild information:   
  * Reason for change: The stepchild questions are very complex and technical, and the current design does not support users in understanding the VA-specific concepts. The new design breaks the stepchild question into more screens to ease cognitive processing, contextually informs users of the rules around stepchildren, and reduces the repetitive collection of information we already have about the user’s spouse in most cases.  
  * Next steps: For next steps and content review status, see the [Add flow changes document in Slack Canvas](https://dsva.slack.com/docs/T03FECE8V/F0A7ZQKKL1M).  
* Federally-funded questions:   
  * Reason for change: The federal funding questions are very complex and technical, and the current design does not support users in understanding the VA-specific concepts. The new design breaks the question into more screens to ease cognitive processing, contextually informs users of the rules around federal benefits, and maps more cleanly to the PDF.  
  * For next steps and content review status, see the [Add flow changes document in Slack Canvas](https://dsva.slack.com/docs/T03FECE8V/F0A7ZQKKL1M).  
* Term dates:   
  * Reason for change: Veteran feedback indicates that this is one of the most confusing sections of the 674\. For this iteration, we are prioritizing trying to bring clarity and better organization to the term date questions that exist on the current form. We hope to do more iterations in the future to help users reporting high school term dates. There is also a team working on updates to the paper form, and we passed along the veteran feedback we collected to them.  
  * For next steps and content review status, see the [Add flow changes document in Slack Canvas](https://dsva.slack.com/docs/T03FECE8V/F0A7ZQKKL1M).  
* Student income:   
  * Reason for change: We know from the 0969 user research that users benefit from more content support for these questions. We added contextual support content that is specific to income and assets that a 18-23 year old are most likely to have. In 674 user research, veterans did not easily recognize the difference between the income pages for THIS year and NEXT year, so we also seek to make that clearer.  
  * For next steps and content review status, see the [Add flow changes document in Slack Canvas](https://dsva.slack.com/docs/T03FECE8V/F0A7ZQKKL1M).  
* Student expenses:   
  * Reason for change: According to the 686 instructions and convos with Pension SMEs, the only thing a user can put in the “REMARKS” section of the form is student expenses. In the current design, this is a free text box that says nothing about expenses. Thus, users are missing out on financial facts that could increase their monthly payments.We want to structure the info we collect about student expenses so that each user has equal opportunity to provide the same info.  
  * For next steps and content review status, see the [Add flow changes document in Slack Canvas](https://dsva.slack.com/docs/T03FECE8V/F0A7ZQKKL1M).

### Phase 2 of Add flow work: Keep/add back student

**Problem it solves**  
The features in this initiative are UX enhancements for users who are trying to update information about a current or previous child dependent that is now a student (18-23). These changes cover a number of areas, including:

* Add new keep/add back option:   
  * Reason for change: Sometimes users have to use the 686 to tell the VA that they need to change the status of a current or previous child to STUDENT or CHILD WITH DISABILITY. They might need to provide school-specific information or disability-related information, but they shouldn’t need to enter the unchanging demographic information about the student/child that the VA already has. Users are confused by the current pattern, where to do this, they have to pick “add dependent”. This design provides users with a more mental-model-oriented option.  
* Question order and backend PDF/BGS mapping changes:   
  * Because users will no longer have to enter information the VA already has when reporting on a student, we will need to prefill the 674 using existing backend info, and update the flow UI so that we don’t show those redundant questions to users

**What we did**  
This work focused on the "keep" scenario of students who were on the benefits as a child, but turning 18, and going to school, so the dependent could stay on the benefits. However, we heard from VA staff and Veterans that another "keep" scenario also proved challenging – children turning 18 who had a permanent disability, and thus must report their disability to VA. We expanded scope to include that use case as well. We created a possible flow and screens to account for these keep use cases, assembled it in a prototype and tested it with Veterans. Coming out of that research, we made some changes to some screens (more to do\!), and have a list of findings and recommendations.

### Phase 3 of Add flow work: Add net-new student

**Problem it solves**  
The features in this initiative are UX enhancements for users who are trying to add a child 18-23 who is in school, and has never been on their benefits before (ex: a stepchild they now have after getting married)

This feature-set should come last (after the first 2 phases), since it is less common than the others, and requires some reordering of the main form steps. These changes cover a number of areas, including:

* Consolidate child add options:   
  * Reason for change: Users who want to add a net-new student have to select 2 contradictory options: add child 18-23 in school, and add child \<18. We want to optimize the experience for these users so the options provided make intuitive sense. In doing so, we will also simplify the steps for ALL users adding a child.  
* Disability questions:   
  * Reason for change: Since we are consolidating the child add types from 3 options to 1, we need a way for the user to indicate whether the child is disabled. We also want to consolidate the 2 disability questions into one to make it easier for the user to answer.  
* Is the child attending school?   
  * Reason for change: To allow users to add a net-new child 18-23, we need some way to know whether the child is in school or disabled (the only 2 conditions that allow an older child to be on the award). Since we are consolidating the ‘add child’ options into one option, we need some other way to get this info from the user. So we want to add in this question that triggers the need for the 674\.  
* Ineligible due to age:   
  * Reason for change: Users may not realize that they can't add a child over 18 if they are not disabled or in school. We want to gently inform users of this rule so that they can correct any data errors they might have made in answering previous questions, and save time if they realize they are not eligible through this process. They should still be able to submit the form if they want.  
* Child marriage:   
  * Reason for change: The 686c and 674 have different requirements for the child’s marriage. Additionally, we do not ask if the child is currently married, a condition that makes a dependent ineligible. Since we are enabling the addition of a net-new student, we want to make sure that we can collect accurate info required by each PDF.  
* Child address:   
  * Reason for change: The 686c and 674 have different requirements for the child’s address. The VA needs more information about a child \<18 than they do for a child \>18. We want to only collect information that the VA actually needs, and reduce the burden on applicants of filling out unnecessary information. This design conditionally displays different address Qs based on the user’s age.

**What we did**  
The team has spent the least amount of time working on this part of the initiative. We've created initial design concepts for each of the screens/problem areas, based on past research and knowledge built up by talking to stakeholders. We had discussed the changes with engineers and validated the approach with our SME David Reis.

**Other notes**

* Based on the proposed flow changes in the keep/add back work, changes may need to be made to this work as it's designed currently.  
* These changes require updates to the question order and PDF mapping (all indicated in the figma file)

**Why it was stopped**

* The team progressed this until the end of the contract period. However, the team hadn't started to move this phase of the project forward beyond initial design exploration.

**Next steps**

* Review designs and flow, based on recent research, and changes to the keep/add back flow.  
  * Make any changes necessary.  
* Review and finalize content internally.  
* Decide whether or not flow/design changes need research with Veterans or collab cycle.  
* Make any more changes necessary.  
* Finalize designs and content with sitewide content team.

### Integrate dependent management info into MyVA

**Problem it solves**  
One challenge in the dependents space is making users aware of the dependents on their award. Users who are seeking this info can find it easily, but those who are not seeking it may not realize when something is wrong.

**What we did**  
The team did initial discovery around how dependent information might be included into both My VA and the Profile on VA.gov. It also did some design explorations around the work, focused on putting a version of the picklist into the Profile.

**Other notes**  
The design team hypothesized that placing a version of the picklist in the Profile could potentially help Veterans see it more often, and know it may need updating regularly.

**Challenges**

* The Authenticated Experience team is evolving both My VA and the Profile, more tightly scoping the purpose for each product. It didn't fit to place a version of the picklist or even a card of a dependent that needs updating in My VA because it's meant to be a place that shows information that Veterans need to take action on. Currently, the DMT team didn't have the APIs available to show what dependents need updating (Diary date for each dependent).  
  * The DMT explored putting a version of the picklist in the Profile, as recommended by the Authenticated Experience team because that's where information about Veterans belongs. However, the experience felt similar to the My dependents page.

**Why it was stopped**  
The team paused this work since the Authenticated Experience team had already moved through the Collaboration Cycle on some simple text changes for the Profile and My VA experiences. The DMT team thought we could influence the experience more in the future when other areas were more built out, such as notifications for My VA.

**Next steps**  
Monitor the work from the Authenticated Experience team, and determine when it makes sense to dedicate resources to helping evolve the dependent experience in My VA and Profile.

### 686/0538 combo work

**Problem it solves**  
The 0538 only includes some removal reasons and doesn't include the ability to add dependents. This means a user could think they are seeing all add/remove options, but they are not. Integrating the 0538 into 686 would allow users to make any changes necessary to their dependents AND satisfy the VA’s verification requirement.

**What we did**

* [A broad discovery effort](https://app.mural.co/t/departmentofveteransaffairs9999/m/departmentofveteransaffairs9999/1745620624724/70ee36c200a3b66b426c33aa189dcdcc5a4a0192?wid=0-1773779191017) into how it might work to combine the 0538 (dependent verification) with the 686/674 (add/remove a dependent/student).  
* We focused on [mapping out a high-level flow](https://app.mural.co/t/departmentofveteransaffairs9999/m/departmentofveteransaffairs9999/1745620624724/70ee36c200a3b66b426c33aa189dcdcc5a4a0192?wid=0-1763522503702) for how this could work, coupled with some pros and cons for how it solves current pain points.

**Other notes**  
The design team hypothesizes that this is a good approach to take because:

* Many Veterans don't care about distinguishing which questions go with which forms on the VA.gov or mobile experience – as long as they know at a high level which forms they are submitting, they just want to accomplish their task.  
* Transitioning between forms creates friction and a chance Veterans won't complete needed information.  
* The Add flow changes tested with Veterans in Feb. 2026 via the 686 set the stage for asking up front if a Veteran's dependents are up to date, making folding in the 0538 easier.

**Why it was stopped**  
Sanja prioritized the Add flow changes ahead of this work because the form combination here had a number of open, complicated questions. Among them:

* How complex would it be to combine the forms on the backend, processing what would now be three forms: 686, 674 and 0538?  
* Would we need to determine if we want to generate an 0538 when a 686 gets submitted, and how we handle that. We do have some options available.

**Next steps**

* Discuss level of effort with engineers.  
* Create a matrix of tradeoffs between user experience, VSR impacts and technical tradeoffs.  
* Determine if research and/or collab cycle would be helpful  
* Determine whether analytics or quant data could help us make a decision or track this feature

### No SSN

**Problem it solves**  
RBPS requires a SSN for all dependents currently, but there are some eligible dependents without a SSN (ex: children, spouses, or parents who are citizens of another country).

**What we did**  
We completed design work for this initiative, and launched it. When it launched, we rolled it out to a small number of Veterans, and noticed that some people who filled out the form selected the "They haven't gotten a SSN yet". After some investigation on the claims filed, we noticed that some of the Veterans had dependents that should have SSNs. We turned off the feature, even though it was functionally working right. We decided that more design work might be needed to make the feature work in a way where Veterans weren't making the wrong decisions.

**Other notes**

* The Slack thread linked below has some more context from David about possible future choices that could be explored further with David Reis.  
* Our hypothesis is that the "They haven't gotten a SSN yet" is too broad, and that's why Veterans with newborn children are selecting that option vs. waiting until the child has a SSN card.  
* It may be good to make changes, and release to a small portion of Veterans, instead of testing in research since getting real-world feedback here is valuable.

**Why it was stopped**

* We launched the feature, but need to keep iterating on it.

**Next steps**

* Talk to David Reis from VBA to understand how choices may need to get expanded.  
* Iterate on designs.  
* Test with Veterans, if necessary.  
* Hand off to engineering.
