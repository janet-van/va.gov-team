# Conversation Guide for Testing Line Charts within Specific Calculator Fields


### Day of the session

> [!TIP]
> - Create a DM group in Slack for real-time feedback from observers.
> - Mute your Slack notifications.
> - Send out the Notetaker and Observer Guide

### Notetaker and Silent Obeserver Guide
- Be a silent observer. If you have a question for the participant, ask the facilitator via a Slack DM and they'll accommodate it if appropriate.
- If you can notetake, please do! It helps researchers a lot to see what you captured. There’s a note taking template in this Mural with screenshots. If you find it easier to write your notes or use a word doc, that's fine too! If you take running notes, please be sure to make a clear reference to the screen in question or the scenario being mentioned.
- Write what you hear and see as much as possible. For some it can be easier to write in first person, as it is said, so that you don't need to paraphrase anything. Try to capture what they said without adding your own interpretation of what they meant. E.g. "I'm not sure about this checkbox" does not equal "Doesn't understand checkbox."
- Share your notes back with the facilitator via Slack if taken outside of the mural. Do not include ANY PII in this chat.
- For design testing, try to write down things that aren't said—for instance, when someone misunderstood some copy or clicked the wrong element. It helps us figure out what needs to be revisited.
- Share a few raw impressions with the facilitator via Slack or in the notetaking document after the interview is done, if possible. It helps with synthesis!

**Protecting PII**
- It's important to protect the privacy of our VHA staff participants AND any Veterans. 
- Do not include the participants name or other identifiers -- such as a very specific title that can be easy to identify them with in your notes. Instead, use a general role description, such as "primary care provider" and "also works in informatics"
- In the event a participant needs to share their screen or a tool with an actual patient (since test patients don't always work to show how the tool functions), stop the recording before they share. DO NOT RECORD THE PARTICIPANT'S SCREEN IF THEY ARE SHOWING A REAL PATIENT.


### Starting the session

> [!TIP]
> - In the Attendees section, make sure everyone except the participant is on mute.
> - In the Participant "more" menu, make sure "Play Join and Leave Sound" is unchecked
> - Check your [screensharing setup] and share permissions for participants.
> - When the participant is ready, begin the session with the following intro*

# Question Bank - 

Before we get into details, I'd like to learn a little bit about you. Can you tell me about your current role at the VA and how long have you been at the VA?

> [!TIP]
> - Let the clinician lead the session as much as possible to share about their experience and pain points using Medical Calculators. Refer to the priority questions if you need to probe if they aren't naturally covering it. It's not expected that all questions are covered in all sessions.

1. I'd like to learn more about your role and how long you have been using Medical Calculators.
2. Please walk me through the last time you used Medical Calculators. What, if anything, was challenging about that experience. Why? What, if anything, was straightforward about that experience? Why?
3. What calculator did you use? At what point in your workflow did you use the calculator? [pre-encounter prep, during the visit, after the visit]
4. What calculators do you use most often? (or What calculators are in your favorites list?
5. What calculators could we add that would provide the most value to your clinical workflow or clinical decision making?
6. How often do you encounter issues with autopopulation? Is it a specific type of field or calculator?
7. How often do you need to refer to clinical information beyond what exists in the "Guidelines" section to help with your decision making? What, if anything, in the clinical content is helpful? What, if anything, is missing that would help with your workflow? Where do you find the clinical information you need to support your decision making?
8. What barriers or pain points do you have when using the medical calculator application?
9. What would help you use Medical Calculators more frequently in your workflow?
10. Review full list of calculators and ones that have little use. How would you use these (or not)? Why do you think the usage is low?
11. How often have you been using Medical Calculators?
12. Can you breifly walk be through what your experience was like the last time you used Medical Calculators?
13. What, if anything, was challenging about that experience? Why?
14. What, if anything, was straightforward about that experience? Why?

## Feature Overview:
Time permitting, highlight a few features based on what you've observed or heard in the previous overview

I want to point out a few things about Medical Calculators that you might not know about. 
- Favorites - If you don't have favorites, you can click the star from the list page or from a calculator page. This will save to a tab and next time you open Medical
- Calculators it will default to this tab. You can always go back to the full list or use Search to look for a calculator that isn't in your favorites.
Patient Details
- This page gives an overview of patient health data and recommends calculators based on Key Conditions. Key Conditions right now is a small list of conditions that are pulled from the problem list. We don't see much usage of this feature. Why do you think that is? (Show Console Patient Details).
- Note preferences - Some calculators have toggles to include more information in your note. The preview option lets you see how it will be formatted to paste into your note.
- Modals - For autopopulated data, we have a link below the field to open a modal that discloses what codes we are mapping to for a specific question.
- New calculator alert
- What are some ways we could point out how these features work in a way that supports your workflow, learning, and isn't obtrusive while trying to get results for your patient?
- What do you think of these features? How would you use them? How helpful (or not) are they to using Medical Calculators?
